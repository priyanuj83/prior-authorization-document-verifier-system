# ORTHOPAEDIC CONSULTATION

**Reason for referral:** Right knee injury, ED attendance 16 September.

**Interval history**

Just over a fortnight since the netball injury. The effusion has largely settled and
her pain is much improved at rest. What has not settled is a **catching sensation** in
the knee, which she describes as the joint snagging on something as she moves. She
notices it several times a week, usually on rotation or when getting out of a car.

She also mentions one occasion about a week ago when the knee "felt stuck for a
moment" — she was standing up from a low chair. It freed itself after a second or two
without her having to do anything to it, and it has not recurred.

Regarding the physiotherapy referral from the ED: she tells me she was contacted by
the department and has been **doing exercises at home** since. She is not entirely
sure how the rest of the course is meant to be arranged and asked me about it today.
I have encouraged her to follow it up with them.

**Examination**

Right knee: trace effusion only, much improved. ROM now full, 0 to 135 degrees,
symmetrical with the left.

Medial joint line tenderness persists, localised to the mid and posterior thirds.
McMurray's positive medially with a reproducible catch and discomfort. Apley's grind
uncomfortable medially.

Ligaments stable — Lachman negative with firm endpoint, drawers negative, collaterals
stable to stress at 0 and 30 degrees.

Quadriceps bulk mildly reduced on the right compared with the left, consistent with
two weeks of relative disuse. Straight leg raise intact.

Distal neurovascular examination normal.

**Assessment**

Symptomatic medial meniscal tear of the right knee is the most likely diagnosis in a
24-year-old following a rotational injury, with a positive McMurray's and persistent
mechanical catching. Her plain films from September showed a structurally normal,
non-arthritic knee, which is what I would expect at her age.

The single episode of the knee feeling briefly stuck is worth noting but it was
transient and self-resolving, and I would not characterise it as true locking. She has
had no episode where the knee has been fixed and required manipulation to release.

**Plan**

1. Continue with the physiotherapy programme. I have again advised her to make contact
   with the therapy department to ensure the course is properly arranged and
   supervised rather than left as home exercises.
2. Analgesia as required.
3. Avoid netball and other pivoting activity in the meantime.
4. Review in four weeks. If the catching persists at that stage we should discuss
   further imaging and consider arthroscopic management.
5. Return sooner if the knee locks and will not release.
