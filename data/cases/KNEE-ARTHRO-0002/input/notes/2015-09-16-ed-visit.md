# EMERGENCY DEPARTMENT NOTE

**Chief complaint:** Right knee injury, twisting.

**HPI**

24 y/o female, fit and well, no significant past history. Injured her right knee this
afternoon during a netball match — went to change direction, the foot stayed planted,
and she felt something give on the inside of the knee with immediate sharp pain. Came
off the court straight away. Swelling has come up over the last few hours.

Pain 6/10, worse with twisting and with deep flexion. Able to weight bear with a limp.
No prior injury to either knee. No previous knee symptoms of any kind.

**ROS:** Negative apart from the above.
**PMH:** Nil.
**Meds:** Nil regular.
**Allergies:** None known.
**Social:** Non-smoker. Plays netball twice weekly, otherwise active.

**Examination**

Comfortable, ambulatory with antalgic gait.

Right knee: moderate effusion. Medial joint line tenderness, well localised. ROM
approximately 5 to 105 degrees, limited by pain and swelling. No patellar
apprehension. Bony landmarks non-tender.

Ligaments: Lachman negative, anterior and posterior drawer negative, varus and valgus
stress stable. McMurray's not attempted today given acute effusion and guarding.

Neurovascular examination normal distally.

Left knee normal.

**Imaging**

Plain radiographs right knee, AP and lateral, obtained.

> Radiologist read: No acute fracture, dislocation or lipohaemarthrosis. Joint spaces
> symmetrical and fully preserved. No marginal osteophyte or subchondral sclerosis.
> Kellgren-Lawrence grade 0. Soft tissue fullness consistent with effusion.
>
> Note that the menisci and cruciate ligaments are not assessable on plain
> radiography. If soft tissue injury is suspected clinically, cross-sectional
> imaging would be required.

**Assessment**

Acute right knee injury, twisting mechanism, medial joint line tenderness with
effusion. Clinically suspicious for medial meniscal injury. Radiographs show a
structurally normal knee with no degenerative change and no fracture.

**Plan**

1. RICE. Simple analgesia — ibuprofen and paracetamol PRN.
2. Tubigrip. Weight bear as tolerated, crutches offered but declined.
3. **Physiotherapy referral placed today** for the right knee.
4. Orthopaedic outpatient follow-up arranged in approximately two weeks.
5. Advised to return if the knee locks and will not straighten, if she becomes unable
   to weight bear, or if the swelling worsens significantly.

Patient counselled and safety-netted. Discharged home.
