# ORTHOPAEDIC FOLLOW-UP

**Interval history**

Four weeks on from her last review, six weeks from the index injury.

The catching has not improved. She reports it is happening about as often as before,
perhaps two or three times a week, and it continues to interrupt her. She has not
returned to netball. She is managing day to day but the knee is not right and she is
frustrated by the lack of progress.

She has continued with exercises in the interim. She has not brought any paperwork
from the therapy department with her today.

No further episodes of the knee feeling stuck. No giving way. No new injury.

**Examination**

Right knee: no effusion. ROM full and symmetrical, 0 to 135 degrees.

Medial joint line tenderness unchanged, still well localised posteromedially.
McMurray's positive medially with the same reproducible catch as at the last visit.

Ligaments stable throughout. Quadriceps bulk now closer to symmetrical.

Distal neurovascular examination normal.

**Assessment**

Persistent symptomatic medial meniscal tear of the right knee in a young patient with
a non-arthritic knee. Six weeks from injury, her mechanical symptoms are unchanged
and clinical examination is unchanged. Non-operative management has not resolved the
problem.

She is a young, active patient with a structurally normal knee apart from the
suspected meniscal injury, and in my view arthroscopic assessment with partial
meniscectomy is now the appropriate next step.

**Plan**

1. Proceed to right knee arthroscopy with partial medial meniscectomy.
2. Risks discussed — infection, thromboembolism, stiffness, incomplete symptom relief,
   and the small possibility of no significant pathology being found. She understands
   and wishes to proceed.
3. Authorisation request to her insurer to be submitted today.
4. Listed for day-case surgery pending authorisation.
5. She will continue her exercises in the interim.
