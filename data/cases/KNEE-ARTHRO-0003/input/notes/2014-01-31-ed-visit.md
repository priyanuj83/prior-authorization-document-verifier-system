# EMERGENCY DEPARTMENT NOTE

**Chief complaint:** Acute worsening of left knee pain.

**HPI**

67 y/o female with a long history of left knee arthritis, presenting after misstepping
off a kerb this morning. She felt a sharp pain on the inner side of the left knee at
the time, different in character from her usual arthritic ache, and the knee has been
more swollen and more painful since.

She has had trouble with this knee for decades — she tells me it was first diagnosed
in her late thirties and she has been under some form of ongoing musculoskeletal
follow-up for it ever since. Her baseline is a persistent deep ache with morning
stiffness, difficulty with stairs, and pain after walking more than a few hundred
metres. She uses paracetamol most days and topical anti-inflammatories.

What is new today is the sharp medial pain and the swelling.

**ROS:** No fever. No calf pain. No numbness or distal weakness.

**PMH:** Osteoarthritis of the left knee, long-standing. Prediabetes.
**Meds:** Paracetamol PRN, topical NSAID.
**Allergies:** None known.
**Social:** Lives alone, independent. Mobilises with a stick outdoors.

**Examination**

Alert, uncomfortable.

Left knee: moderate effusion superimposed on an obviously arthritic joint. Palpable
bony irregularity around the joint margins. Varus alignment on standing. Crepitus
throughout range.

New, well-localised tenderness along the medial joint line, which she confirms is the
site of today's sharp pain and is distinct from her usual diffuse discomfort.

ROM 10 to 95 degrees, with a fixed flexion deformity of about 10 degrees which she
says is long-standing. Ligaments stable to stress. Neurovascular examination normal
distally.

Right knee: also crepitant with reduced range but not symptomatic today.

**Imaging**

Plain radiographs left knee, AP, lateral and skyline, obtained in department.

> Radiologist read: No acute fracture or dislocation.
>
> There is **severe tricompartmental degenerative change**. The medial compartment
> joint space is completely obliterated with **bone-on-bone contact** between the
> medial femoral condyle and the medial tibial plateau. Large marginal osteophytes
> are present at the medial and lateral femoral condyles, the tibial plateaux and the
> patella. Marked subchondral sclerosis with several subchondral cysts medially.
> Varus alignment.
>
> Overall appearances are those of end-stage osteoarthritis, **Kellgren-Lawrence
> grade 4**.
>
> Soft tissue swelling consistent with the clinically noted effusion. Menisci are not
> assessable on plain radiography.

**Assessment**

Acute-on-chronic left knee pain in a 67-year-old with radiographically end-stage
osteoarthritis of that knee. Today's new medial joint line tenderness following a
twisting misstep raises the possibility of a superimposed degenerative meniscal tear
on top of the established arthritis.

No fracture. No evidence of septic arthritis — she is afebrile and the joint is not
hot.

**Plan**

1. Analgesia. Continue paracetamol, add short course of oral NSAID with gastric cover
   given her age.
2. Ice, elevation, relative rest. Continue using her stick.
3. **Physiotherapy referral placed today** for the left knee.
4. Orthopaedic outpatient follow-up in approximately two weeks.
5. Return if unable to weight bear, if the knee becomes hot or she becomes febrile,
   or if the swelling increases significantly.
