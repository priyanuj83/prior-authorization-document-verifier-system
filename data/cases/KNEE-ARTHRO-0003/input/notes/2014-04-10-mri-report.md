# MRI LEFT KNEE WITHOUT CONTRAST

**Clinical indication:** Persistent mechanical catching following twisting injury
January 2014, on a background of established osteoarthritis. Query meniscal tear.
Referred by Mr H. Castellanos.

**Technique:** Multiplanar multisequence imaging of the left knee at 1.5T. No contrast.

**Comparison:** Plain radiographs left knee 2014-01-31.

## Findings

**Menisci.** There is a **complex degenerative tear of the posterior horn of the
medial meniscus**, with an oblique component extending to the inferior articular
surface. The meniscal body shows diffuse intrasubstance signal change consistent with
degeneration, and there is **medial meniscal extrusion** of approximately 4 mm beyond
the tibial margin. The tear is not displaced and there is **no bucket-handle
configuration**. The meniscal root attachments are intact. The lateral meniscus shows
mild degenerative signal change without discrete tear.

**Articular cartilage.** **Full-thickness cartilage loss** over the weight-bearing
surface of the medial femoral condyle and the medial tibial plateau, with exposed
subchondral bone over an area of approximately 2.5 cm. Reactive subchondral marrow
oedema and small subchondral cysts in the medial tibial plateau. Cartilage loss of up
to 80 percent thickness in the lateral compartment and grade 3 change in the
patellofemoral compartment. Overall **Outerbridge grade 4** in the medial compartment.

**Osteophytes.** Extensive marginal osteophytosis at the medial and lateral femoral
condyles, tibial plateaux and patella, corresponding to the radiographic findings.

**Cruciate ligaments.** Anterior and posterior cruciate ligaments intact.

**Collateral ligaments.** Intact. Mild medial soft tissue thickening.

**Other.** Moderate joint effusion. Small popliteal cyst. Varus alignment. No fracture.
No loose body.

## Impression

1. **Complex degenerative tear of the posterior horn of the medial meniscus** with
   meniscal extrusion. No displaced or bucket-handle fragment.
2. **Advanced tricompartmental osteoarthritis, most marked medially, with
   full-thickness cartilage loss and exposed subchondral bone.** These findings
   corroborate the end-stage appearances on the January radiographs.
3. Cruciate and collateral ligaments intact.

**Comment.** The meniscal tear is degenerative in nature and occurs in the context of
advanced compartmental cartilage loss rather than in an otherwise structurally normal
knee. The severity of the underlying arthritis is likely to be the dominant
contributor to her overall symptoms.
