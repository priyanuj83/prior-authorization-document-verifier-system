# ORTHOPAEDIC CONSULTATION

**Reason for referral:** Left knee, acute-on-chronic pain, ED attendance 31 January.

**Background**

Reviewing her record, this lady has carried a diagnosis of left knee osteoarthritis
since **June 1986** — some twenty-eight years — and has had an open musculoskeletal
care plan for it over that entire period. Her arthritis is well established and is
managed conservatively with analgesia and activity modification.

**Interval history**

Ten days on from the kerb incident. The effusion has partially settled and the
constant sharp pain has eased somewhat.

However she now describes a **catching sensation** in the knee, which she is clear is
new and quite different from her usual stiffness. She describes the joint snagging
mid-movement, most often when she turns while standing at the sink. It has happened
several times a week since the injury. She is emphatic that this is not the same as
the crunching and stiffness she has lived with for years.

She denies any episode where the knee has locked fixed and required her to manipulate
it to release. It catches and then frees itself.

She has begun attending the physiotherapy department following the referral from the
emergency department.

**Examination**

Left knee: small residual effusion. Marked bony enlargement of the joint margins,
palpable osteophytes medially and laterally. Coarse crepitus through range. Varus
alignment. Fixed flexion deformity approximately 10 degrees, long-standing.

ROM 10 to 100 degrees.

Medial joint line tenderness remains focally reproducible in the posterior third,
distinguishable on examination from the diffuse peri-articular tenderness of her
arthritis. McMurray's positive medially with a reproducible catch, though the
manoeuvre is limited by her fixed flexion.

Ligaments stable. Quadriceps bulk reduced bilaterally, consistent with long-standing
arthritis and reduced activity.

Distal neurovascular examination normal.

**Assessment**

Two things are going on in this knee and it is worth separating them.

First, she has end-stage tricompartmental osteoarthritis of the left knee, grade 4 on
the January radiographs, with bone-on-bone contact medially. This is a
twenty-eight-year-old problem and is the dominant structural finding.

Second, superimposed on that, she has new mechanical catching and focal posteromedial
joint line tenderness following a twisting injury, which is suggestive of a
degenerative meniscal tear.

**Plan**

1. Continue with the physiotherapy programme. I have asked the department to work
   through a full course with her and to send me a summary at the end rather than
   discharging her early.
2. Continue analgesia. Avoid prolonged NSAID use given her age.
3. Review once the therapy course is complete. If the catching persists we will image
   the knee to characterise the meniscus.
4. I have begun a discussion with her about the longer-term picture. Given the state
   of the joint, arthroplasty is the intervention that would address her overall knee
   problem, and she should start thinking about it. She is not keen at present and
   would prefer to explore something less major first.
