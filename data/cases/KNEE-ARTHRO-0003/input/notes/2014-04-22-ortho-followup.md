# ORTHOPAEDIC FOLLOW-UP

**Interval history**

Reviewed following completion of her therapy course and the MRI.

She finished with the physiotherapy department at the start of this month. Their
summary confirms she attended twice weekly and completed the full course, that her
strength and walking tolerance improved, but that the catching persisted unchanged
throughout — including during sessions in their department.

Three weeks on, the catching continues. She reports it two or three times a week. Her
background arthritic pain is somewhat better controlled than it was in January, which
she attributes to the therapy.

**Examination**

Left knee: trace effusion. ROM 10 to 100 degrees, unchanged. Coarse crepitus. Marked
bony enlargement, varus alignment, fixed flexion deformity as before.

Focal posteromedial joint line tenderness persists. McMurray's remains positive
medially with a reproducible catch. Ligaments stable.

Quadriceps bulk improved from February, consistent with the therapy gains.

**Imaging review**

MRI of 10 April confirms a complex degenerative tear of the posterior horn of the
medial meniscus with extrusion, no displaced fragment. It also confirms advanced
tricompartmental disease with full-thickness medial cartilage loss and exposed
subchondral bone, Outerbridge grade 4, corroborating the January radiographs.

**Assessment and discussion**

She has a confirmed degenerative medial meniscal tear producing mechanical catching
that has not responded to a completed supervised therapy course.

I discussed the options with her at length today, and I want to record the discussion
honestly.

The difficulty is that the meniscal tear sits within a knee that has end-stage
arthritis. The radiologist's comment that the arthritis is likely the dominant
contributor to her symptoms is reasonable. Arthroplasty is the intervention that
matches the structural problem, and I raised it again.

She remains reluctant. She is independent, lives alone, and is anxious about the
recovery from a joint replacement. She has asked whether something can be done about
the catching specifically, since it is the symptom that bothers her most day to day
and it is the one that is new.

On that basis, and at her request, I am proposing arthroscopic partial medial
meniscectomy to address the mechanical symptom. I have been explicit with her that
this will not treat her arthritis, that her baseline arthritic pain will very likely
be unchanged, and that she may still need arthroplasty in due course. She accepts
this and wishes to proceed on those terms.

**Plan**

1. Left knee arthroscopy with partial medial meniscectomy, at the patient's request,
   to address mechanical catching.
2. Risks discussed, including the specific risk that arthroscopy in a knee with
   advanced arthritis may produce little symptomatic benefit and may accelerate
   symptoms. She understands.
3. Authorisation request to be submitted. Therapy summary, MRI and radiograph reports
   to accompany it.
4. Arthroplasty discussion to be revisited at her next review regardless of the
   outcome of this request.
