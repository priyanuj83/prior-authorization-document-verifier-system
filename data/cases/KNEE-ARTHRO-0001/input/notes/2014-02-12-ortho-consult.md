# ORTHOPAEDIC CONSULTATION

**Reason for referral:** Left knee injury, seen in ED 31 January.

**Interval history**

Twelve days since the index injury. Effusion has settled somewhat. The pain has
changed character — less constant ache, more intermittent and sharp, localised to the
posteromedial joint line.

The important new development is that the knee has begun **catching and then locking
outright**. He reports this has now happened four or five times since the injury,
typically when he rises from a seated position or pivots. On each occasion the knee
becomes stuck in partial flexion and he has had to reach down and manually straighten
the leg before it will release. This is unmistakable to him and quite alarming. It has
happened twice in the last week alone.

He also describes a sensation of the knee wanting to give way on stairs, though he has
not actually fallen.

He has started with the physiotherapy service and has attended his initial assessment
and a handful of sessions since.

**Examination**

Left knee: small residual effusion. Full extension achievable, flexion to 125 degrees,
mildly restricted compared with the right at 140.

Medial joint line tenderness persists and is well localised to the posterior third.
McMurray's manoeuvre reproduces a palpable click with pain medially. Thessaly test at
20 degrees positive for medial discomfort.

Ligaments: Lachman negative with firm endpoint. Anterior and posterior drawer
negative. Varus and valgus stress stable at 0 and 30 degrees. No posterolateral
rotatory instability.

Quadriceps bulk symmetrical, no wasting. Straight leg raise intact against
resistance. Distal neurovascular examination normal.

**Assessment**

Left knee medial meniscal tear following a twisting injury in a 33-year-old with an
otherwise structurally normal knee. Radiographs from 31 January showed preserved joint
spaces with only minimal marginal change, so there is no meaningful degenerative
disease in this knee to complicate the picture.

The episodes of the knee becoming stuck and requiring manual manipulation to release
are a genuine concern and are consistent with an unstable meniscal fragment. I would
note these are true locking episodes and not simply pain-limited motion.

**Plan**

1. Continue with the physiotherapy programme as arranged. I have asked the therapy
   team to persist with a structured course rather than discharge him early, and to
   send me a summary once the course is complete.
2. Simple analgesia as required.
3. Avoid pivoting sports for now. He may cycle and swim.
4. Review once the therapy course has finished. If the locking episodes continue at
   that point we will need cross-sectional imaging to define the tear before
   considering anything operative.
5. He understands that if the knee locks and will not unlock he should attend
   urgently.
