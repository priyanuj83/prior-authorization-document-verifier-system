# PHYSIOTHERAPY DISCHARGE SUMMARY

**Referral source:** Emergency Department, referral received on the date of his
attendance there following the basketball injury.

**Body region treated:** Left knee.

**Course of treatment**

Mr Goyette was seen for initial assessment here in the department shortly after his
referral came through, and has attended twice weekly since, coming in to the clinic for
each appointment. He completed **16 attended sessions** in total across the course.
Attendance was excellent — he missed only one booked appointment, which was
rescheduled the same week.

Programme content:

- Early phase: effusion management, quadriceps activation, closed-chain range of
  motion within pain limits.
- Middle phase: progressive resistance work for quadriceps and hamstrings, hip
  abductor strengthening, proprioceptive retraining on unstable surfaces.
- Late phase: attempted return to controlled multidirectional loading.

Each session was delivered here under direct supervision with hands-on assessment at
every visit. He was additionally given a between-visit exercise sheet, which he
reported completing consistently, but this was an adjunct to the supervised programme
rather than a substitute for it.

**Outcome**

Objectively his quadriceps strength has improved and is now symmetrical with the
right, and his effusion has fully resolved.

However there has been **no sustained improvement in his primary complaint.** The
mechanical episodes have continued throughout the entire course of treatment. He
reported locking episodes in most weeks that he attended, including in the final week,
and on two occasions the knee locked during a session here in the department and
required manual unlocking before we could continue. Attempts to progress him to
multidirectional loading were consistently limited by this, not by strength or pain.

He remains unable to return to pivoting sport and reports the knee is not reliable on
stairs.

**Discharge status**

Discharged from the therapy programme today, having completed the course as
requested by the orthopaedic team. Further therapy is unlikely to alter the mechanical
symptoms, which in my view are structural rather than a strength or control deficit.

Summary copied to Dr Danvers as requested.
