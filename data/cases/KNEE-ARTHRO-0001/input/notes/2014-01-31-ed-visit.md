# EMERGENCY DEPARTMENT NOTE

**Chief complaint:** Left knee injury.

**HPI**

33 y/o male, previously well, presents this evening after twisting his left knee
during a pickup basketball game roughly two hours ago. He describes planting the
left foot and pivoting, felt a pop, and had immediate pain on the inner side of the
knee. Unable to continue playing. Ambulated off the court with assistance. Denies
prior injury to this knee. No knee problems before tonight.

Pain 7/10 on arrival, worse with weight bearing and with attempts to squat.

**ROS:** Otherwise negative. No fever, no numbness, no distal weakness.

**PMH:** Nil of note.
**Meds:** None.
**Allergies:** None known.

**Examination**

Alert, comfortable at rest.

Left knee: moderate effusion. Tenderness along the medial joint line. Range of
motion limited by pain and swelling, roughly 10 to 100 degrees. No bony tenderness
over patella, fibular head, or femoral condyles. Ligament testing limited by guarding
but no gross laxity — anterior drawer and Lachman feel stable, varus and valgus
stress stable. Neurovascularly intact distally, DP and PT pulses 2+, sensation normal.

Right knee unremarkable.

**Imaging**

Plain radiographs left knee, three views, obtained in department.

> Radiologist read: No acute fracture or dislocation. No lipohaemarthrosis.
> Joint spaces well preserved throughout. Minimal marginal changes only,
> Kellgren-Lawrence grade 1. Alignment normal. Soft tissue swelling consistent with
> effusion.

**Assessment**

Acute left knee injury with medial joint line tenderness and effusion following a
twisting mechanism. Clinically suspicious for medial meniscal injury. No radiographic
evidence of fracture. Radiographs do not exclude soft tissue injury.

**Plan**

1. Knee immobiliser, crutches, weight bear as tolerated.
2. Ice and elevation. Paracetamol and ibuprofen PRN.
3. **Referral placed today to the outpatient physiotherapy service** — patient to
   commence a supervised programme for the left knee. Appointment booking to be made
   by the therapy department directly with the patient.
4. Orthopaedic clinic follow-up in approximately two weeks.
5. Return sooner if the knee becomes locked, if he cannot bear weight at all, or if
   he develops fever.

Discussed with patient, understands and agrees.
